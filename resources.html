Resources

Things that might help

A curated list of support services, reading, and other resources for people navigating grief. We've tried to keep this practical and honest about what each one is.

If you need help right now

DPS is a peer support group — we're not a crisis service. If you're struggling and need to talk to someone urgently, please reach out to one of these services.

📞
Lifeline — 13 11 14 24/7 crisis support and suicide prevention. Call, chat online, or text 0477 13 11 14.
💙
Beyond Blue — 1300 22 4636 Support for anxiety, depression and related conditions. Available 24/7 by phone, chat, and email.
🧠
headspace Mental health support specifically for young people aged 12–25. Centres across Australia and online options.

Grief support services

These organisations specialise in bereavement and loss — some offer individual counselling, some run their own groups.

🌿
Australian Centre for Grief and Bereavement Education, counselling, and support for those experiencing grief. Based in Victoria with national reach.
🌿
Grief Australia Free grief support services for Australians, including telephone support and referrals to local counsellors.
🌿
National Association for Loss and Grief (NALAG) Advocacy, resources and support connecting Australians with local grief services.

Reading on grief

Books that DPS members have found genuinely helpful — not prescriptive, not self-helpy in the bad way.

A raw, precise account of grief after sudden loss. Widely loved for its refusal to offer tidy comfort. Not specifically about parental loss, but the experience it describes is deeply recognisable.

Written by a therapist who lost her partner — this book pushes back on the idea that grief is a problem to be solved. Validating and practical. Good for people who are tired of being told they're grieving wrong.

A landmark book specifically about losing a mother in young adulthood. Draws on interviews and research to explore how this loss shapes identity and relationships over decades. A companion volume, Motherless Mothers, also exists.

A memoir by a neurosurgeon facing terminal illness — written from the perspective of someone who knows they are dying. Offers a different angle on loss: what it means to be both the dying person and those left behind.

About building resilience after loss. More research-based than emotional in its approach — useful for people who find the data helpful, less so for those wanting to sit with the feeling.

Practical support

Sometimes grief intersects with practical matters that nobody prepares you for. These resources can help.

📄
MoneySmart — Managing a loss ASIC's guide to managing finances, estates, and accounts after a death. Plain-language and genuinely useful.
📄
Services Australia — Bereavement payments Information about Centrelink bereavement payments and other financial support available to those who have lost a family member.

Know something we've missed?

This list is curated by our community. If you've found something genuinely helpful that isn't listed here, we'd love to hear about it.
