About us

Why we exist

DPS started because of a simple realisation: losing a parent in your 20s puts you in a strange in-between place, and most support structures weren't built for it.

Our story

Dead Parents Society began in Canberra as a small gathering of friends who'd each lost a parent and kept finding themselves having the same conversation — that particular kind of grief that sits alongside first jobs, new relationships, and the slow work of becoming an adult without someone who was supposed to witness it.

We started meeting monthly. Other people heard about it and wanted to come. And it turned out a lot of people needed exactly this: not a support group in the clinical sense, not a therapy session, but a room full of people who just got it.

The name came from that same spirit of honesty. We're not trying to soften it. Our parents are dead. We're figuring out how to live with that. And we're doing it together.

What we believe

🕯️

Grief doesn't have an expiry date

Whether your parent died last month or a decade ago, you're welcome here. There's no timeline you need to be on.

🔓

Honesty over positivity

We don't require silver linings. You can be angry, or sad, or oddly fine. We make space for all of it.

🪴

Peer support is real support

There's something uniquely powerful about being heard by someone who has been through the same thing. We're not a substitute for professional help — we're a complement to it.

🌏

Community builds over time

We're starting in Canberra and growing. Every new chapter is started by someone who found something here worth sharing.

Who is DPS for?

DPS is for people roughly aged 18–30 who have lost a parent or someone who played a significant parental role in their life — a step-parent, grandparent who raised them, guardian, or other figure.

You don't need to identify as a "griever" or be in active distress. You might be mostly okay and just want a place where people understand your specific experience. That's enough.

Please note: DPS is a peer support group, not a clinical mental health service. If you're in crisis or need professional support, we've listed some helpful services on our Resources page.

Questions?

We'd love to hear from you. Reach out any time — whether you're thinking about coming along, want to get more involved, or just want to say hello.
